10 Red Flags: How to Spot Phishing

  1. Sender Domain Mismatch: Display name claims to be a brand, but the actual domain address differs (e.g. PayPal Support from invalid.test).
  2. Artificial Urgency: Demands immediate action within 12 or 24 hours to create panic and bypass rational review.
  3. Deceptive Hyperlinks: Hovering reveals numerical raw IPs (198.51.100.10) or URL shorteners hiding destination servers.
  4. Direct Credential Requests: Demands passwords, PINs, or MFA keys via unsecured external forms.
  5. Weaponized Double Extensions: Disguised executables such as Invoice.pdf.exe or Statement.scr.
  6. Depersonalized Greetings: Uses generic openers like "Dear Customer" or "Attention Staff" instead of personalized names.
  7. Financial Coercion: Claims overdue payments, threatening collections agencies or legal penalties.
  8. Unexpected Wealth or Prizes: Claims you won lotteries, sweepstakes, or cryptocurrency giveaways you never entered.
  9. Unusual Grammar or Casing: ALL-CAPS screaming subjects, multiple exclamation marks, or unnatural phrasing.
  10. Contextual Mismatch: Inquiries about systems or vendors your department does not use.
